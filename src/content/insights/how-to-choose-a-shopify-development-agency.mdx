Hiring a Shopify development agency is one of the biggest decisions an online store makes. The right partner can make your store faster, easier to buy from, and simpler for your team to run. The wrong one leaves you with a slow theme, a pile of apps, and a store nobody on your team can update.

This guide explains what a Shopify agency actually does, what to look for, the questions to ask before you sign, and the warning signs to avoid.

## What a Shopify development agency does

A Shopify agency designs, builds, and improves stores on Shopify. Most work falls into a few groups:

- **New store builds:** setting up a new Shopify store, usually on an Online Store 2.0 theme, with your products, pages, and checkout settings.
- **Theme customisation:** changing an existing theme so product pages, collections, and the home page fit your brand and products.
- **Migrations:** moving from WooCommerce, Magento, BigCommerce, or another platform without losing products, customers, orders, or search rankings.
- **Apps and integrations:** connecting Shopify to your ERP, inventory, accounting, email, or CRM tools, or building a custom app when nothing off the shelf fits.
- **Speed and conversion work:** removing apps you no longer need, fixing slow pages, and improving the path from product page to checkout.

Some agencies only build. Others also run marketing such as Shopping ads, email flows, and SEO. Decide early which you need, because it changes who you should hire.

## Signs you need an agency, not a template

A theme and a few apps are enough for many new stores. An agency starts to pay off when:

- Your store is slow and you're not sure which apps or code are the cause.
- Product pages don't answer customers' questions, so they leave or email you instead.
- You're moving from another platform and can't risk losing search traffic.
- You need Shopify to talk to other systems, such as stock, orders, or invoicing.
- You sell in several countries, languages, or currencies.

## What to look for in a Shopify agency

**Real, live Shopify stores in their portfolio.** Ask for links, not just screenshots. Open them on your phone. Are they fast? Is it easy to find a product and reach checkout?

**Experience with stores like yours.** Selling 20 products is very different from selling 2,000. Ask about stores with a similar catalogue size, price point, and number of markets.

**A clear process.** A good agency explains how they move from discovery to design, build, testing, and launch, and what they need from you at each step.

**Honest advice about apps.** Every app adds monthly cost and can slow the store. A good agency will often suggest fewer apps, not more.

**Care for SEO during changes.** Redesigns and migrations can lose rankings if old URLs aren't redirected. Ask exactly how they protect your search traffic.

**You own everything.** The store, the theme code, the domain, and the app accounts should all stay in your name.

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. Can you show me two or three live Shopify stores you built or improved?
2. Will you customise an existing theme or build a custom one, and why?
3. Which apps would you add, remove, or replace in my store?
4. How will you keep my store fast after launch?
5. How do you handle redirects and SEO during a redesign or migration?
6. Who will I talk to day to day, and how often will I get updates?
7. What exactly is included in the quote, and what would cost extra?
8. What happens after launch? Is there support, and how is it billed?
9. Will my team be able to edit pages and products without a developer?
10. Who owns the theme code and accounts when the project ends?

## How much a Shopify agency costs

There's no single price, because the work varies so much. These are the main things that change the cost:

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Theme or custom build | Customising a good theme costs far less than building one from scratch |
| Number of unique page designs | Each new template adds design and build time |
| Catalogue size and data | Large catalogues need careful imports, filters, and metafields |
| Migration | Moving products, customers, orders, and URLs takes planning and testing |
| Integrations | Connecting ERP, stock, or accounting tools is often the most technical part |
| Markets and languages | Each extra market adds content, currency, and testing work |

Ask for a written quote that lists what's included. If a price seems very low, check what's missing, such as testing, redirects, or training.

## Warning signs to avoid

- **No live examples**, only mock-ups or screenshots.
- **Guaranteed sales or conversion numbers.** Nobody can promise results before seeing your store.
- **A long list of paid apps** for problems that could be solved in the theme.
- **No mention of redirects** when you're redesigning or migrating.
- **Vague scope**, such as "full store build" with no list of pages or features.
- **They keep the accounts or code** in their name instead of yours.

## Theme customisation or a custom storefront?

Most stores are best served by a well-customised Shopify theme. It's faster to build, cheaper to run, and your team can edit it. A custom (headless) storefront makes sense only for unusual needs, such as very complex content or performance targets a theme can't reach. If an agency pushes headless straight away, ask them to explain why a theme won't work.
