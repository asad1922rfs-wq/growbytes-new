Most growing businesses eventually ask the same question: should we invest in search engine optimization or paid advertising first? Both can bring the right visitors to your website, but they work on different timelines, cost money in different ways, and suit different situations.

This guide explains how each one works, when each tends to fit, and what to fix before spending on either.

## How they differ

**SEO** improves how your website appears in unpaid search results. It involves technical health, useful content that matches what people search for, and a site structure search engines can understand. Results build gradually, and well-structured content can keep bringing visitors after the work is done.

**Paid advertising** places ads in search results or social feeds, usually charging per click or per impression. Traffic can start quickly once campaigns are approved, but it generally stops when spending stops.

| | SEO | Paid advertising |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Builds over months | Can start within days |
| Cost model | Time and expertise up front | Ongoing spend plus management |
| When spending pauses | Existing results tend to persist for a while | Traffic from ads stops |
| Best for testing offers | Slow to give feedback | Fast feedback on messages and pages |

Neither is better in general. The right starting point depends on your situation.

## When paid advertising is the better first step

Paid campaigns often make sense when:

- **You need inquiries soon,** for example for a launch or a seasonal service
- **You are testing a new offer** and want to learn which messages and pages convert before investing in content
- **Your market is small or specialist,** with a manageable number of relevant searches
- **You have a clear way to measure results,** such as tracked form submissions or orders

Paid advertising rewards preparation. Sending visitors to a slow or unclear page wastes budget, so landing pages and tracking should be ready before campaigns start.

## When SEO is the better first step

SEO often makes sense when:

- **People already search for what you offer,** and those searches have clear intent
- **You can commit to steady improvement** over several months rather than expecting quick results
- **Your website has technical problems** that also hurt every other channel, such as slow pages, broken links, or pages search engines cannot read
- **Advertising costs in your sector are high** relative to the value of a new customer

Much of SEO is simply good website practice: clear page structure, fast loading, and content that answers real questions. That work tends to improve conversion from every source, including ads.

## Fix these before spending on either

Whichever you choose, a few foundations make the difference between useful data and wasted budget:

1. **Tracking:** make sure inquiries, calls, and orders are recorded correctly, so you can see what each channel produces.
2. **A clear page for each offer:** visitors should land on a page that matches what they searched for, not a general homepage.
3. **Page speed and mobile experience:** many visitors arrive on phones; slow or awkward pages lose them regardless of how they arrived.
4. **A simple next step:** an obvious form, booking option, or phone number, with as few fields as possible.

## Using both together

Many businesses use both, with each informing the other. Paid campaigns can quickly show which search terms and messages lead to inquiries. Those findings help decide which content to build for SEO. Meanwhile, improvements made for SEO, such as faster pages and clearer content, tend to make paid campaigns more efficient.

A practical approach for a modest budget is to fix the foundations first, run a focused paid campaign to learn what converts, and invest in SEO content around the topics that prove valuable.

## Questions to ask before deciding

- How soon do we need new inquiries?
- Do we know which services or products bring the most valuable customers?
- Can we currently see where inquiries come from?
- Is the website fast, clear, and easy to use on a phone?
- Can we commit to ongoing work for several months?
