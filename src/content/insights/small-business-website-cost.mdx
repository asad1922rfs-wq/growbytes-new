Ask ten people what a small business website costs and you'll hear ten different numbers. That's not because anyone is hiding the price. It's because "a website" can mean a one-page template or a full system that brings in leads every day. This guide breaks the cost into clear parts, so you can see what you're paying for and set a budget that fits.

![There are three common ways to get a business website, and cost rises with how much is done for you.](/images/insights/website-cost.svg)

## The three ways to get a website

**1. Build it yourself with a website builder.** Tools like Wix, Squarespace, or WordPress.com give you templates and hosting for a monthly fee. The main cost is your time. It works well for testing an idea or a very simple site.

**2. Hire a freelancer.** One person designs and builds the site. This can be good value for a simple brochure site. The risk is that everything depends on one person's skills and availability.

**3. Work with an agency.** A team handles planning, content structure, design, development, and SEO. It costs more, and it makes sense when the website is a main source of leads or sales.

## What published price ranges say

Many guides publish typical ranges. For example, [WebsiteSetup's 2026 guide](https://websitesetup.org/how-much-does-a-website-cost/) puts a professionally built small business site in the low thousands of dollars, with simple template sites costing far less and complex builds costing much more.

Treat any range as a rough guide, not a quote. Your price depends on the points below.

## What actually changes the price

- **Number of pages and templates.** Ten pages that share one layout cost less than ten pages that each need their own design.
- **Content.** Writing, photos, and video take real time. If you supply ready content, the project is faster.
- **Custom design vs template.** A template is quicker. Custom design fits your brand and your visitors better.
- **Features.** Booking, online payments, member areas, multiple languages, or connections to your CRM all add work.
- **SEO and migration.** Moving an old site without losing Google traffic needs redirects and careful checks. It's worth it, but it's part of the cost.
- **Who edits it later.** Setting up an easy-to-edit CMS and training your team takes some time up front and saves a lot later.

## The running costs after launch

A website isn't a one-time purchase. Plan for:

| Cost | What it is |
|---|---|
| Domain | Your web address, renewed every year |
| Hosting | The server your site runs on. Some platforms include it |
| Business email | Usually a separate monthly fee per mailbox |
| Plugins or apps | Some features charge a monthly or yearly fee |
| Updates and fixes | Security updates, small changes, and new pages |

Small costs add up, so write them down before you choose a platform.

## Cheap vs good value

A cheap site that doesn't bring in enquiries is expensive. A good-value site does three things well:

1. Visitors understand what you do within a few seconds.
2. Every page has one clear next step, like "Get a quote" or "Book a call".
3. It loads fast and works well on a phone.

If a quote doesn't mention these, ask how the site will turn visitors into enquiries.

## Questions to ask before you hire anyone

- What exactly is included, and what costs extra?
- Who writes the content and who supplies photos?
- Will we be able to edit pages ourselves?
- Who owns the site, the domain, and the hosting account?
- What happens after launch if something breaks?
- How will you protect our current Google rankings?

Clear answers to these tell you more than the price alone.

## How to plan your budget

Start with what the website needs to achieve, not with the number of pages. Then list the must-have features, the content you already have, and your running costs.
